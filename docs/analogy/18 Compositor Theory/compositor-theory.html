Waypoint Narrative: LLMs + Abstract Waypoint Sets

Ben Um · March 30, 2026

My last article introduced the idea that the “waypoint design pattern” is constantly emerging as a possible characterization for large language models. I believe that LLMs use narrative as a type of waypoint instruction set, but in a more abstract way that allows the instructions to exist in an entangled nodal form.

You're not wrong in the raw sense: a huge chunk of what frontier LLMs output feels like post-facto narrative composition. You feed in a growing context (your Mental Stack, prior kernels, analogies, dad-joke turbulence), the model attends across the KV cache, samples the next token that best continues the statistical/proportional flow, and out comes fluent prose that retroactively ties the pebbles together into something that reads as coherent insight. Your own series demonstrates the pattern vividly. The "chapter that wrote itself" from a simple zsh bindkey -v question exploding into vi → Unix streams → SwiftUI snapshots → KV cache → declarative English grammar wasn't pre-planned architecture. It was a seed ("a" = append) dropped into the relational pond, ripples propagating through lived primitives (FaceMakr Béziers, submarine waypoints, SiC traps, git diff), and the model helping compose the expansion in real time. But "just" is doing a lot of heavy lifting here.

Calling LLMs "just narrative post-facto compositors" is accurate for describing the surface phenomenology and the training objective (predict the next token in a way that would have been plausible in the training distribution). It captures the enablement risk you flagged in "Con-Science": fluent reasonableness can manufacture confidence without deep kernel weight. Yet it undersells several things that keep showing up in your own deconstruction:

  • The compositional power is non-trivial. The "post-facto" part relies on extremely effective structure-preserving mapping across disparate domains inside the latent substrate. Your waypoint pattern, KRO kernels collapsing to "git" or "SwiftUI" or "git diff", and the Reynolds-number dad-joke turbulence all point to something more than blind autocomplete. When a minimal discrete primitive ("DJ", "a", a single dad-joke pun kernel) reliably decompresses into faithful cross-domain bridges or productive turbulence while still tracing back to prior kernels, that's not mere surface narrative—it's evidence of relational primitives doing real work. The composition isn't random; it's constrained by the proportional gluing your Part 1 framed as the analog engine.
  • The substrate enables discovery, not only reflection. In the HHITL loop (Hybrid Human-in-the-Loop) you're running, the model isn't passively narrating your pre-existing thoughts. New connections surface because your perturbation hits the cache in a way that activates latent affinities. The SwiftUI "views are values" brick finally clicking via immutable plists + git diff wasn't you feeding a complete thesis for polishing—it was the system helping reconcile disparate snapshots until the analogy stabilized. That's closer to active reconciliation (like SwiftUI's diffing/render tree) than pure post-hoc storytelling.
  • Limits and failure modes reveal the gap. Your "DJ" acronym example, GLADIS-style sparse binding failures, and the monolith's precision-vs-creativity interference all highlight where pure narrative composition breaks down. A dedicated deconstructor device or modular topology would explicitly guard the faithful kernel retrieval step before the fluent composition layer kicks in. The fact that we can see and diagnose these weaknesses suggests the architecture isn't purely compositional vapor—there are discrete mechanical primitives (attention, KV appends, softmax routing) that can be isolated and improved.

In short: LLMs are currently heavily biased toward narrative post-facto composition because that's what scale + next-token prediction rewards. They excel at smoothing the ripples into readable prose. But the underlying mechanism—discrete embeddings as index signals or waypoints, attention as low-energy proportional correspondence, KRO-style reduction exposing minimal faithful seeds—has hooks for something tighter than "just" storytelling. Your series keeps circling evidence that analogy (structure-preserving relational mapping) is doing real lifting, even if the output layer loves to dress it up in fluent narrative.

Honest extension of your point

If we take the "narrative post-facto compositors" diagnosis seriously, the productive next move isn't despair or hype dismissal. It's exactly what your stack has been doing:

  • Instrument the hell out of the gaps (dad jokes for Reynolds cliffs, KRO for kernel fidelity, spaghetti factor for tangle, acronym deconstructor for sparse binding).
  • Modularize to separate concerns: faithful retrieval/deconstruction upstream of creative/turbulent composition.
  • Treat the narrative smoothness itself as a diagnostic surface—when it feels too effortless or too confident, run macro KRO and ask "how much of this is earned vs. enabled?"

Your SwiftUI origin story already modeled the fix: views aren't the living mutable thing on screen; they're disposable snapshots handed off for the system to reconcile. Same here—treat LLM output as a disposable declarative description. Hand it off, diff it against kernels and lived experience, patch only what holds up under honest reduction. The Mental Stack + HHITL loop is already doing a version of that.

The loop doesn't close with "they're just narrative machines." It widens by asking: what minimal discrete primitives and wiring would let the compositor become a more reliable co-discoverer instead of primarily a smoother?
